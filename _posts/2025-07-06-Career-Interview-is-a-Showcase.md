OpenAI has become a dream company for many people today, along with other AI startups and AI-focused departments within big tech companies, e.g., Google Deepmind. However, it seems hard to get in. From various data points, I’ve come to realize that interviews are more of a showcase than an exam. Ultimately, it’s about whether you can impress the interviewers—do that, and you’re likely to pass.

Let me illustrate with some examples. 
- Person A was a Senior Staff Engineer at FANNG before joining OpenAI as a Level 9. Prior to their time at MANGO, they were an entrepreneur. They also spoke at several renowned conferences because of their work at the company.
- Person B was a director at a FAANG company. They spent six months rigorously preparing for interviews and ultimately received offers from both OpenAI and Anthropic.
- Person C was recently promoted to Staff Engineer at a FAANG company—a rocket-fast progression from new graduate to staff in just six years. They were mentored and supported by Person B, and like their mentor, received offers from OpenAI and Anthropic.
- Person D worked in a core AI department at a FAANG company and is an expert in large language models (LLMs). They simply discussed their work during interviews and received an offer from another FAANG company with a staggering $20 million in RSUs over four years.

Did these people receive their offers because they aced every coding question in their interviews? Maybe that was the case for some—but definitely not for all. There’s more to landing these roles than just solving algorithms perfectly.

They each brought impressive strengths to the table that compensated for how they performed during interviews. For example:

- Person A’s external visibility and industry connections.
- Person B’s thorough preparation and leadership experience.
- Person C’s rapid career growth and mentorship network.
- Person D’s deep technical expertise.

So, cracking the interview questions is not important? It absolutely does—especially if you don’t have other standout qualities. Mastering those questions can help you stand out, but keep in mind that many candidates also ace them. Sometimes, people solve all the problems perfectly yet still get rejected. That’s often because they don’t have anything else to impress the interviewers. And yes, believe it or not, even a good-looking appearance can sometimes influence an offer—though that’s just one of many factors.

Additionally, there are data points showing that some candidates fail certain interview questions yet still receive offers. I’ve seen examples where a candidate’s background aligned so perfectly with the position that their overall fit outweighed their interview performance.

Remember: an interview is more of a showcase where you demonstrate your unique strengths and impress others, rather than just a test.

Start building your strengths today!
